# Devlog

## Where the game stands

First Impact is a tower defense with a deck-building twist, closer to a roguelike card game than a classic td. You build and upgrade turrets to stop waves of enemies from reaching the goal, but between waves you also shop for cards — new towers, obstacles, or relics — and put together a small stack of passive and active effects that carry you through a run. Nine different turret types cover the usual roles: a cheap basic shooter, a splash cannon, a long range sniper, an anti-air missile launcher, a laser with continuous damage, an ice tower that slows things down, a support tower that buffs its neighbors instead of attacking, and a couple more with their own quirks. Enemies aren't fixed types either — they're built out of combinable traits (tough boss, fast runner, regenerating, flying, splitting on death, bonus reward) so a single wave can mix several behaviors at once.

Runs are organized around a campaign map: you pick a node, play it, and whatever you earn feeds into a persistent meta-progression that sticks around between runs — currency and unlocks that make future attempts a little easier. There's also a full map editor for building and sharing custom layouts, with the usual undo/redo and save/load.

The whole game world moved from flat 2D to a real 3D scene. The map is a small hilly terrain that reacts to actual sunlight, with a day/night cycle that shifts the lighting, the shadows, and now even the sky color to match. Paths are carved into the ground with a soft sunken look instead of being painted on top, water and biome-specific terrain (desert dunes, cracked mountain rock, grassy plains) get their own subtle animated touches, and towers/enemies cast real shadows that follow the terrain's shape instead of floating on top of it. Selecting a tower shows its range as a soft glowing disc that hugs the ground properly, even on slopes and path edges, rather than a flat circle punched through the world. The camera can now be rotated freely around the map with the middle mouse button, always pivoting around the map's center, and zoom always pulls toward that same center point too.

On top of the visuals there's a layer of "juice" — particle effects, screen shake, recoil on towers that fire, squash on enemies that get hit, a frosted-glass look on the pause menu — meant to make combat feel more responsive. There's a small library screen that catalogs every tower and relic by rarity, and tooltips throughout the shop and hand explain what each card does.

A run currently runs for 20 waves per map. Settings include the usual audio/video knobs plus a toggle to show or hide an FPS counter (F9), which now renders with the same font and scale as the rest of the interface. The game supports English, Spanish, and Portuguese.

Under the hood, a good chunk of recent work has been spent trimming leftovers from the transition away from the old 2D renderer and an earlier, differently-shaped version of the shop — dead UI code, unused constants, and a couple of stray features that had lost their way in were found and either removed or reconnected, including a "play a saved map" option that had quietly lost its menu button along the way.

## Latest update

Every generic placeholder on the map got replaced with a real, hand-modeled prop. Each biome now has its own tree — a leafy plain tree, a pine, a curved palm with drooping fronds, a squat mountain bush — plus lily pads drifting on the water, one in four blooming into a two-layer lotus flower. Map obstacles turned into actual buildings: a tile-roofed cottage, a log cabin built from real stacked logs, a low adobe house, and a cluster of angular rocks for the mountain biome, all sized and centered properly instead of floating or sprawling off their tile. Bridges got a suspension-bridge look, with posts, a drooping main cable, and hanging suspension wires instead of a plain fence. The supply plane dropping loot crates is now a real F-16 flying a full pass across the map — nose, canopy, delta wing, tail fin and all — instead of a flat sprite, and it actually flies off past the edge of the map after the drop instead of vanishing the instant the crate falls; the crate itself is a real wooden box with a plank pattern, and both it and the plane now cast proper shadows.

A handful of smaller readability and polish fixes rode along: the road through each biome now uses a warm, worn-dirt color with real contrast against the ground instead of a barely-darker tint of the same hue (desert paths in particular used to nearly disappear into the sand), the shoreline foam animation got slowed down and softened so it reads as a gentle shimmer instead of a fast pulse, and the selection ring under a chosen tower is a touch more transparent.
